## 1. MacStories (John Voorhees)

**To:** voorhees@macstories.net
**Subject:** Elapsa: passport and visa reminders that count back from the six-month rule (indie, out soon)

```
Hi John,

I'm an indie developer, and Elapsa, my iPhone app that tells you when to start renewing a passport or visa rather than when it expires, is in App Review now. I'll release it as soon as Apple approves it, likely within the next week.

It started when we nearly missed a trip. While copying passport numbers into a booking form I noticed one was about to expire, and then learned that many countries refuse boarding with under six months left, and that renewal can take weeks. The printed date isn't the real deadline.

Elapsa gives each kind of document its own renewal lead time and flags every passport that falls below the six-month rule. Scanning reads the passport's machine-readable zone on the device with Apple's Vision framework and checks every check digit, so what's saved is what's printed. There's no account, no server and no analytics; documents are encrypted on the phone and move to a new phone by direct transfer.

Calendar reminders and most expiry trackers remind you about the printed date. Elapsa counts back from when you need to start.

- iPhone, iOS 15 or later, 10 languages

## 3. TidBITS (Adam Engst)

**To:** ace@tidbits.com
**Subject:** PITCH: Elapsa – iPhone app for passport and visa renewal reminders

```
Hi Adam,

Elapsa is an iPhone app that tells you when to start renewing a passport, visa or ID card, rather than when it expires.

The problem: many countries won't let you board or enter with less than six months left on a passport, and renewal can take weeks or months. So the date printed in the passport isn't the deadline that matters, and most people don't check until they book a trip. I found this out when one of our family's passports turned out to be about to expire while I was filling in a booking form.

How it differs from what's already out there: a calendar entry or a general expiry tracker reminds you about the printed date. Elapsa gives each kind of document its own lead time (passports months ahead, visas 90 days, residence permits 120 days), flags every passport below the six-month rule, and reminds you daily in the final month. Scanning reads the machine-readable zone on the passport with Apple's Vision framework, entirely on the device, and verifies every check digit, so it doesn't save a wrong number. There's no account, no server and no analytics; documents are encrypted on the phone.

I designed Elapsa and wrote it with heavy help from Claude Code; the problem, the design and every decision are mine.

Details:
- App: Elapsa 1.0 (Elapsa: Passport, Visa & ID on the App Store)
